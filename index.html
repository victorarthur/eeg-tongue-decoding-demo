Tongue movement decoded from EEG

Video examples for the article “Noninvasive decoding of tongue movement from scalp EEG during speech using ultrasound tongue imaging”.

Each clip is the recorded ultrasound of the tongue during one test trial, with two contours drawn on it and with the speech of the trial as its sound.

  • The tongue contour that a tracker found in the ultrasound.
  • The contour that the decoder produced from the EEG.

English words

A Hungarian sentence

The three clips are readings of one sentence, “A férfi a fejére szorította két karját.” In English: “The man pressed both arms to his head.”

What a clip shows

Each clip is one test trial, a word or a sentence that the decoder did not see in its training. The picture is the ultrasound of the tongue in the mid-sagittal plane, with the front of the tongue to the right.

The decoder reads only the EEG, 59 scalp electrodes and four electrodes around the eyes, within 0.77 s of each frame. It is a small network trained for each speaker, and it predicts the tracked contour of every ultrasound frame.

A clip runs from the first frame after the start beep to the end of the sound recording of the trial.

The recordings belong to the dataset ds007920 on OpenNeuro (under review, so its page does not open yet), doi:10.18112/openneuro.ds007920.v1.0.0. The speakers are named as in the article.
